- The caller's prior of ~4-5 M tons/yr containerboard exports could NOT be confirmed or refuted in this run.

### A6. Apparent consumption by US box plants
Not published in readable sources for 2023-2025. Identity: consumption = production - exports + imports - inventory change.
Inputs found: 2024 production ~37.8 M (derived), imports 1.40 M; exports NOT FOUND. FBA-audited consumption series (members' plants; appears to undercount the whole industry): 2019 32.30 M, 2020 33.07 M, 2021 34.0 M tons (Section B3). A ratio-based estimate for 2024 is 381 BSF x (34.0 M/416 BSF) = ~31 M tons (derived, L). Treat 31-35 M tons as the plausible 2024 range; needs the export figure to pin down.

### A7. Linerboard vs medium split
No official tonnage split found. Evidence: 2024 liner production +5.3% vs medium +3.4% (AF&PA Q4-2024); 2025 semichem medium -1%, recycled liner -2%. Market-research share: linerboard 61.9% of containerboard market 2024 (GMI, revenue basis, https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/containerboard-market) [L]. The caller's ~2:1 (liner:medium) prior is consistent with that but NOT confirmed by AF&PA data in this run. PCA example: Wallula W3 = 285k tpy recycled liner + medium; W2 = 140k tpy medium, idled May 2025 (https://ir.packagingcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/packaging-corporation-america-announces-reconfiguration-wallula) [H].

## E. Logistics

### E1. Shipping radius and mode
- No containerboard-specific radius or modal split was found in readable sources (the targeted query hit the search limit). Qualitative: mills ship "large rolls of paper" to their own converter/box-plant network (https://www.internationalpaper.com/resources/recycling/white-paper/circular-life-cardboard-box); integrateds supply own box plants "as much as possible" and use swap/barter arrangements, sending board to a nearby competitor's box plant to save freight (http://www.paperstudies.org/pricetool/references/Containerboard%20Definitions.pdf) [M].
- General freight economics (use as proxy): rail cost-effective for hauls >400 miles; truck for short, time-sensitive, door-to-door (https://www.rsilogistics.com/blog/comparing-the-costs-of-rail-shipping-vs-truck/) [M]. Example lane Houston-Cleveland: rail direct ~$70.27/net ton, rail+transload ~$95.54, truck ~$214.96 (https://www.rsilogistics.com/blog/comparing-the-costs-of-rail-shipping-vs-truck/ ; https://tratics.com/blog/optimizing-shipping-costs-with-tratics-rail-vs-truck-comparison) [M]. Per ton-mile: rail 4-9 c (2-4 c unit train) vs truck 8-20 c (2026) (https://steelwheellogistics.com/blog/rail-vs-truck-freight-cost-comparison) [L-M].
- AAR: ~700,000 carloads/yr of pulp & paper products on US railroads (https://www.aar.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Overview-of-Americas-Freight-RRs.pdf) [M]. At ~70-90 tons/carload that is ~50-60 M tons/yr of ALL pulp & paper by rail (derived, L).
- CFS 2022 (all commodities): trucks 68.1% of tonnage; 56.4% of tonnage moves <50 miles (https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/cfs-shipments-of-goods.html) [H, not paper-specific].
- Fastmarkets price articles cite "higher manufacturing and transport cost" and fuel as 2025-26 cost drivers (qualitative) (https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/linerboard-pricing-increase-june-containerboard-market-demand/).
